Start with something you have noticed in the city. It could be a surface, an informal use of space, a trace of maintenance or a relationship between plants and built structures. Choose a place where you could make further observations.

## From an interest to a category

A research interest is broader than a visual label. A project about maintenance might start with visible categories such as exposed material, painted surface and plant growth. The model learns only these labels; what they say about maintenance is for your group to interpret.

Set two or three images beside each proposed definition. Include one case where the group disagrees. Discuss which visible features you are using, what the category excludes and what remains difficult to represent.

## Choose a reading

Choose one starting point.

| Starting point | Passage to explore in relation to your project |
|---|---|
| Bowker and Star, [*Sorting Things Out*, introduction](Bowker_and_Star_-_Sorting_Things_Out_Intro.pdf) | How a classification is organised and what its categories make visible or leave out |
| Haraway, [*Situated Knowledges*](Haraway-Situated-Knowledges.pdf) | How a position, instrument or way of observing shapes an account |
| Drucker, [*Humanities Approaches to Graphical Display*](https://digitalhumanities.org/dhq/vol/5/1/000091/000091.html) | How selection and interpretation enter data and its visual presentation |

The other PDFs in this folder are for later exploration.

## Reading with AI

Give the assistant the source text, ask for an explanation, and return to the passage together.

> Explain this passage in plain English, using only the text supplied. Identify the author's claim and distinguish it from your own interpretation. Point to the relevant passage or page where available. Suggest a connection to our proposed categories, and identify one part of that connection the text does not establish.

Keep the passage, your interpretation and a note of anything you corrected in the AI response. If the source is missing, ask the assistant to say so rather than invent a quotation or page reference.

## Bring to the group tutorial

Prepare one page and a small image sheet:

- Your urban interest and a possible place to observe.
- One idea from a reading, with the source passage.
- Two or three category definitions, examples and an ambiguous case.
- One or two search phrases for each category.
- One decision you would like the class to discuss.

A film reference or early visual idea is welcome.
